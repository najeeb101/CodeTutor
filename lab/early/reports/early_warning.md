# Early warning: who may struggle later? (Week 8 Step 3a)

Generated by `scripts/early_warning.py report` (`make early`). Do not edit by hand.

**The question.** At the end of week 3, which students will solve fewer than
40% of the problems they first try *after* that week on the first
try? That share is the label. It is read from the submissions table (outcomes), never from the
simulator's persona labels. The cutoff and the 40% were fixed
before any result was seen.

**Inputs.** Only submissions made up to the cutoff (a test pins that later rows cannot leak
in): first-try share, problems tried, tries per solved problem, activity trend, days since the
last submission, and a Rasch skill fitted on the first tries so far.

**Model.** Logistic regression in pure Python, standardised inputs, a fixed L2 penalty of 1.0.
It is fitted on class seed 0 and scored on the other class. Nothing was tuned on the
class it is scored on. Two simple rules are scored beside it, because a model only matters if
it beats them. A teacher looks at the top 10 students, so precision and recall are for
those 10 (no probability threshold to tune).

## What the model learned (end of week 3)

| input | weight (per standard deviation) | a higher value means |
|---|---|---|
| `tries_per_solved` | 0.94 | more at risk |
| `first_try_share` | -0.78 | less at risk |
| `rasch_skill` | -0.77 | less at risk |
| `days_since_last` | 0.32 | more at risk |
| `activity_trend` | 0.26 | more at risk |
| `problems_tried` | 0.00 | no effect |

Weights of correlated inputs (first-try share and Rasch skill measure nearly the same thing) can trade off against each other, so read the signs loosely, not the sizes.

## Students who stopped coming

| seed | stopped before the last week | of them in the model's top 10 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 6 | 1 of 10 |
| 1 | 6 | 0 of 10 |

The label is about *how well* a student does later, not whether they come back. In the simulator a student who will quit behaves like an average student until the week they stop, so no model can see it coming; "no submission for a week" catches them only after the fact. This report does not claim to predict dropping out.

## Reading the numbers
- Seed 1, end of week 3: model AUC 0.94 vs first-try rule 0.92 (chance 0.50); the difference is inside the first-try rule's interval: no proof the model is better than the simple rule. Top 10: 8 of 10 flagged students are really at risk, against 8 for the first-try rule.

## Limits

- The classes are simulated. Success is drawn from a student's skill, so a rule that looks at
  past success is *expected* to work well. Nothing here says it would work on real students.
- Few at-risk students (14 at risk in seed 1) and 60 students: the intervals are
  wide and one student moves precision by 10 points.
- The model is more than 0.01 AUC worse than the simple first-try rule at: end of week 2 (seed 1). With 6 inputs and about a dozen at-risk students it can fit noise.
- A high score is a reason for a teacher to check in, never a verdict. The simulator's
  learners never change after a warning; a real warning changes the future it predicts.
